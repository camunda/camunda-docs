docs/components/concepts/clusters.md: In Camunda Hub, a cluster is an administrative unit: the infrastructure that organization admins create, size, and maintain. Teams don't deploy to a cluster directly. They deploy to an [environment](./environments.md) hosted on it.

docs/components/concepts/clusters.md: In SaaS, organization admins create clusters in Camunda Hub. When you create a cluster, you choose its type, size, region, and version. The type defines the availability and uptime of the cluster, and the size defines its capacity.

docs/components/concepts/clusters.md: In Self-Managed, you provision clusters outside Camunda Hub, and you don't create them in Camunda Hub. To show a cluster and its environments in Camunda Hub, add it to the Camunda Hub configuration.

docs/components/concepts/environments.md: | :------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

docs/components/concepts/workspaces.md: A workspace is a collaboration space in Camunda Hub. It represents a team or business domain, and it brings together the people, the [projects](./projects.md), and the [environments](./environments.md) that the team works with.

docs/components/concepts/workspaces.md: - **Environments**: A `payments-dev` environment for testing, and a `payments-prod` environment that an organization admin assigned to the team.

docs/components/concepts/workspaces.md: An environment can be assigned to more than one workspace, and a workspace can have any number of environments, including none. Workspace admins and editors see the environments of their workspace. Viewers and commenters don't.

docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ## Permissions
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: You can assign environments to a workspace if you're an **Organization owner** or **Organization admin**. A DevOps user can also manage the assigned environments of a workspace if they have the **Editor** or **Workspace admin** role in that workspace.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ## Assignment rules
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - A workspace can have any number of environments, including none.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - A workspace can have more than one environment for the same lifecycle phase. For example, you can assign multiple development environments to a workspace.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - All projects in a workspace inherit the environments assigned to the workspace.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - An environment can be assigned to more than one workspace. Assigning one environment to one workspace is the recommended model, but Camunda Hub doesn't enforce it.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - Workspace members see only the environments assigned to their workspace. Viewers and commenters see none. See [environments](/components/concepts/environments.md#who-can-see-and-use-environments).
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ## Assign environments when you create a workspace
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 1. In Camunda Hub, click **Workspaces** in the left navigation, and then click **Create workspace**.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 2. Complete the **General** and **Members** steps. See [create a workspace](../manage-workspaces/manage-workspace.md#create-a-workspace).
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 3. Under **Environments**, select the environments for the workspace. You can change this later.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 4. Click **Create workspace**.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: If Camunda Hub creates the workspace but can't assign the environments, it shows a message. Assign the environments from the workspace settings.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ## Change the assigned environments
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 1. In Camunda Hub, click **Workspaces** in the left navigation, find the workspace, and click **Manage**. Alternatively, open the workspace and click **Settings** in the left navigation.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 2. Open the **Environments** tab.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 3. Click **Edit environments**. If the workspace has no environments, click **Add environments**.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: 4. Select the environments to assign, and remove the ones you no longer need. Then click **Save**.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: Saving replaces the assigned environments with your selection.

docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: The selection view lists the available environments. For each environment, it shows the version, status, region, and the other workspaces that use it, or **Not assigned**.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - Use **Search environments** to search by name.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: - Filter by version or tag.

docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ### Remove the last environment
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: If you remove every environment from a workspace, Camunda Hub asks you to confirm with **Remove environments**. The workspace then has no environment to deploy to. You can assign environments again at any time.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: ### What happens when you unassign an environment
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: When you unassign an environment, projects in the workspace can no longer select it, and they can't deploy to it. Nothing else changes in the environment or in other workspaces that use it.

docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: Use the Camunda Hub API to assign environments as part of your workspace setup or team onboarding automation. The API follows the same rules as the user interface.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | Method | Path                                      | Description                                                                |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | :----- | :---------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | `GET`  | `/environments`                           | List the environments in your organization.                                |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | `GET`  | `/workspaces/{workspaceKey}/environments` | List the environments assigned to a workspace.                             |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | `PUT`  | `/workspaces/{workspaceKey}/environments` | Replace the environments assigned to a workspace with the ones you send.   |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: | `GET`  | `/projects/{projectKey}/environments`     | List the environments a project can use. This is the set of its workspace. |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md: The `PUT` request takes an `environmentIds` list of up to 500 environment IDs, and replaces the complete set. To unassign an environment, send the list without it. To unassign all, send an empty list.

docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: ### Environment statuses
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: The status of an environment reflects the state of its cluster. The status updates automatically while the cluster changes state, and settles on the health of the cluster when the change completes.
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Status      | Description                                                                                         |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | :---------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Healthy     | The environment is running.                                                                         |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Unhealthy   | The environment reports a problem.                                                                  |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Unknown     | Camunda Hub can't determine the status.                                                             |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Creating    | The cluster is being created. You can't deploy to the environment until the cluster is ready.       |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Updating    | The cluster is being updated. You can still deploy to the environment, but the deployment may fail. |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Unavailable | The cluster is under maintenance or waiting for input. You can't deploy to the environment.         |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Paused      | The cluster is paused. You can [resume](#resume-a-paused-environment) the environment.              |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: | Resuming    | The cluster is starting after being resumed.                                                        |
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: #### Resume a paused environment
docs/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/index.md: Organization owners, admins, and DevOps users can resume a paused environment wherever Camunda Hub shows its status, for example on the **Environments** page, in the environment details, or when you select an environment to deploy to. The status changes to **Resuming** while the cluster starts, and then to **Healthy**.

docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ## Cluster type
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: The cluster type defines the Availability Target for the cluster.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: You can choose from three different cluster types:

docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **Standard**: A production-ready cluster with a higher Availability Target.

docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ## Recovery objectives by outage type
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: Camunda 8 SaaS sets Recovery Time Objective (RTO) and Recovery Point Objective (RPO) targets for each type of outage, based on how much infrastructure the outage affects. They don't vary by cluster type. These objectives, including those in the **Recovery objectives summary** table and the RTO/RPO assessments for each type of outage below, describe expected behavior on a best-effort basis. They aren't contractual commitments and are separate from the Availability Target.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Availability Target versus recovery objectives
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: The Availability Target and recovery objectives measure different things. Keep them separate when you plan for disaster recovery.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **The Availability Target** is a contractual commitment. It measures the percentage of minutes in a calendar month during which the Orchestration Cluster is available, based on connection checks by Camunda's monitoring system, as defined in your agreement with Camunda. It doesn't describe what happens during or immediately after an outage.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **RTO (Recovery Time Objective)** measures how long a failure disrupts service, from the moment it starts affecting your cluster until the cluster is fully functional again. For outages that need manual recovery, RTO also includes the time to detect, escalate, and diagnose the problem.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **RPO (Recovery Point Objective)** measures how much data you can lose when that failure happens, expressed as the time between the last recoverable point and the failure.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: A high Availability Target doesn't imply a fast recovery or minimal data loss during a major infrastructure failure. The Availability Target tells you how rarely a failure disrupts your cluster; RTO and RPO tell you how well the cluster recovers when a major failure does happen.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Recovery objectives summary
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Outage                                | RPO                            | RTO                                          | Recovery                            | Requirement                                            |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | :------------------------------------ | :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Node                                  | Zero                           | Near zero                                    | Automatic                           | None                                                   |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Availability zone                     | Zero                           | Near zero                                    | Automatic                           | None                                                   |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Region                                | Time since the restored backup | Depends on data volume and reconnection time | Manual cold recovery that you start | Dual-region backup location in a supported region pair |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Cloud provider or third-party service | Not defined                    | Not defined                                  | Depends on the provider's recovery  | Not available                                          |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: | Platform or cluster incident          | Typically zero                 | Depends on the incident                      | Camunda incident response           | None                                                   |
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: Near zero means service resumes automatically, typically within seconds, without a backup restore. Requests in progress during that time can fail, so configure your clients and job workers to retry them, for example with exponential backoff.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: Your application's overall recovery time also depends on your own job workers and clients being able to reach the cluster and continue processing.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Node failure
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: A node failure is the loss of a single Zeebe broker or [secondary storage](/reference/glossary.md#secondary-storage) node within a cluster. Camunda SaaS clusters replicate each partition across multiple brokers, typically one broker per availability zone. When a single broker fails, the remaining brokers take over its partitions automatically, without data loss. Secondary storage is managed by Camunda and also replicated, so losing a single secondary storage node doesn't lose data. To learn more, see [clustering](/components/zeebe/technical-concepts/clustering.md).
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **RTO/RPO assessment:** RPO is zero, because every committed record is already stored on the remaining nodes. RTO is near zero, and clients recover through their standard retry mechanisms.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **Your responsibilities:** Configure your clients and job workers to retry failed requests. Run job workers across multiple availability zones so that the same outage doesn't disrupt them.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Availability zone failure
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: An availability zone (AZ) failure is the loss of an entire zone in the cluster's region, taking every broker hosted there down at once. All cluster types keep three copies of your data, one in each of three availability zones, so losing one zone leaves the cluster fully operational.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **RTO/RPO assessment:** RPO is zero, because the remaining zones already hold every committed record. RTO is near zero. Failover is automatic, doesn't require a restore, and clients recover through their standard retry mechanisms. While the zone is unavailable, the cluster runs with reduced redundancy. A second failure in another zone before recovery can make the cluster unavailable.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **Your responsibilities:** Configure your clients and job workers to retry failed requests. Run job workers across multiple availability zones so that the same outage doesn't disrupt them.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Region failure
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: A region failure is the loss of every availability zone in the cluster's region at once, for example, during a regional cloud provider outage. Camunda SaaS clusters run in a [single region](/components/saas/regions.md). By default, backups are stored in the same region as the cluster. If you select a [dual-region backup location](/components/saas/backups.md#backup-location), backups are also replicated to the secondary backups region. For [supported region pairs](/components/saas/cross-region-cold-recovery.md#supported-region-pairs), you can then use [cross-region cold recovery](/components/saas/cross-region-cold-recovery.md) to recover the cluster in the secondary region.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **RTO/RPO assessment:** Recovery from a region failure is a manual cold recovery that you start in Console or the API. Camunda creates a new cluster in the recovery region and restores the backup you select. Without a dual-region backup location in a supported region pair, recovery to another region isn't possible.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **RPO** is the time between the backup you restore and the failure. It depends on your backup schedule, on backups being created and replicated successfully, and on the restore point you select. A 15-minute backup schedule doesn't guarantee a 15-minute RPO.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - **RTO** is the time needed to detect the outage, start failover, restore the backup, and reconnect your applications. The more data your cluster holds, the longer the restore takes, which increases the RTO. The recovered cluster has new endpoints, so you must update your DNS or client configuration. Preparing network infrastructure in the recovery region in advance reduces the RTO.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **Your responsibilities:** Choose a dual-region backup location in a supported region pair when you create the cluster, and keep your backup schedule healthy. Prepare network infrastructure in the recovery region in advance. After failover, point your clients and job workers at the recovered cluster, and don't use the original cluster again. For the full procedure, see [fail over](/components/saas/cross-region-cold-recovery.md#fail-over).
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Cloud provider or third-party service outage
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: An outage at Camunda's cloud provider, network and edge providers, or other third-party services can make clusters unreachable, or prevent Camunda from starting or replacing infrastructure, until the provider recovers. Camunda SaaS clusters run on a single cloud provider and can't fail over to another provider.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: This differs from a region failure. If an outage affects only your cluster's region and your secondary backups region is still available, you can recover through cross-region cold recovery. If the outage also affects the secondary backups region, or prevents Camunda from creating infrastructure there, the cluster stays unavailable until the provider recovers.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **RTO/RPO assessment:** Clusters stay unavailable until the provider recovers, so Camunda doesn't set an RTO or RPO for this type of outage. Camunda follows its incident response process and publishes updates on the [Camunda status page](/components/saas/status.md).
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: ### Platform or cluster incident
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: Some incidents originate in Camunda's own platform or in a single cluster. Examples include platform configuration issues that affect network access, software defects, and cluster components in an inconsistent state.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: **RTO/RPO assessment:** RTO depends on how quickly the incident is detected, escalated, diagnosed, and resolved, so Camunda doesn't set a fixed RTO. RPO is typically zero, because these incidents usually affect availability, not stored data.

docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - Larger cluster sizes include increased performance and capacity, allowing you to serve more workload.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - Increased usage such as higher throughput or longer data retention requires a larger cluster size.
docs/components/saas/clusters.md: - Each size increase uses one of your available cluster reservations. For example, purchasing two HWP advanced reservations for your production cluster allows you to configure two clusters of size 1x, or one cluster of size 2x.

docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: #### Deployments target environments instead of clusters
docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: Starting with Camunda 8.10, teams deploy to [environments](/components/concepts/environments.md) instead of the clusters connected to a project. An environment is a named deployment target where a team runs its processes, and it's hosted on a cluster. Clusters remain the infrastructure your administrators manage.
docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: - Projects no longer have their own deployment stages or connected clusters. A project can deploy to every environment assigned to its workspace.
docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: - Organization admins assign environments to workspaces.
docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: - In Self-Managed, Camunda Hub creates environments from the clusters in your `camunda.hub.clusters` configuration, and from any Physical Tenants you declare.
docs/reference/announcements-release-notes/8100/8100-announcements.md: **Action:** After you upgrade, assign environments to the workspaces you create. Optionally, tag a cluster with `prod` if you want Camunda Hub to treat its environments as production environments for the project deployment policy.

docs/reference/glossary.md: ### Environment
docs/reference/glossary.md: An environment is a named deployment target where a team runs [processes](#process) in [Camunda Hub](/components/hub/index.md). It's the operational unit for deployment, while the [cluster](#cluster) is the administrative unit. An environment is backed by a [Physical Tenant](#physical-tenant) on Self-Managed 8.10 and later, or by the whole cluster on SaaS and on earlier versions. Organization admins assign environments to workspaces, and every project in a workspace can deploy to the environments assigned to it.

docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: #### Mark a cluster as production
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: This step is optional. Tag a cluster with `prod` only if you want Camunda Hub to treat its environments as production environments. Camunda Hub treats an environment as a production environment if the tags of its cluster include `prod`. The match is exact and case-sensitive, so `prod` works but `Prod` and `production` don't. All Physical Tenants of a cluster tagged `prod` are production environments. See the [project deployment settings](/components/hub/workspace/modeler/modeler-settings.md#project-deployment).
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: ```yaml
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: camunda:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:   hub:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:     clusters:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:       - id: camunda-platform
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:         # other fields...
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:         tags: ["prod"]
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: ```
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: ### Physical tenants
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: Declare the [Physical Tenants](/self-managed/concepts/multi-tenancy/physical-tenants.md) of your clusters in the Camunda Hub configuration. Camunda Hub surfaces each declared Physical Tenant, and the `default` Physical Tenant of every cluster, as an [environment](/components/concepts/environments.md) that teams deploy to. An environment appears only if its cluster is in your configuration. Camunda Hub reads the cluster configuration once at startup on every instance, so after you change it, perform a rolling restart.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: The version of the cluster decides which Physical Tenants Camunda Hub surfaces as environments:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | Cluster version   | Environments                                                                                                                                                                               |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | :---------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | 8.10 or later     | One for the `default` Physical Tenant, which always exists, plus one for each Physical Tenant you declare under `physical-tenants`. Camunda Hub names the `default` one after the cluster. |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | Earlier than 8.10 | One environment for the whole cluster, named after the cluster.                                                                                                                            |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: If you declare `physical-tenants` on a cluster earlier than 8.10, Camunda Hub ignores them and logs a warning.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: #### Declare physical tenants

Camunda Hub uses the ID of a tenant as the name of its environment, except for the `default` tenant.

docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: #### Override components for a physical tenant
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: Use `components` on a Physical Tenant to point it at its own component instances. This is a partial override:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: - Only the component types you list are replaced for the tenant. A listed component replaces the cluster entry completely, so set every address the tenant needs, such as `urls.webapp` and `urls.readiness`.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: - Every other component keeps using the configuration of the cluster, and follows later changes to it.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: - A tenant other than `default` never inherits Optimize from the cluster, because Optimize needs its own instance for each Physical Tenant. Add an `optimize` component to the tenant to show Optimize.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: - If you remove the override and restart Camunda Hub, the component uses the configuration of the cluster again.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: Example configuration that overrides only Optimize for the `payments-prod` tenant. The other components still come from the cluster:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: ```yaml
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: camunda:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:   hub:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:     clusters:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:       - id: camunda-platform
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:         # other fields...
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:         physical-tenants:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:           - id: payments-prod
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:             components:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:               - type: optimize
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:                 version: 8.10.0
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:                 urls:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:                   webapp: https://optimize-payments-prod.example.com
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md:                   readiness: https://optimize-payments-prod.example.com/api/readyz
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: ```
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: If a cluster earlier than 8.10 declares `components` on a tenant, Camunda Hub fails to start with the message `must not declare physical tenant 'components' if 'version' is lower than the minimum physical tenant version`.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: #### Environment status
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: Camunda Hub sends an HTTP request to the `urls.readiness` address of each component of an environment to determine its status. The status of the environment is the worst result of its components:
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | Component response                                                                                      | Status    |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :-------- |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | A successful response, with no body or with a `status` of `up` or `ready`                               | Healthy   |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | An error response, or any other `status`                                                                | Unhealthy |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: | No `readiness` address, no response within five seconds, a redirect, or a body without a `status` field | Unknown   |
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: A cluster that you configure with `url` instead of `components` has no readiness address, so its environments always have the status **Unknown**.
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: #### Not reported environments
docs/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md: If you remove a cluster or Physical Tenant from the configuration, but its environment is still assigned to a workspace, the environment stays in Camunda Hub with the status **Not reported**. It shows no live data, and you can't select it for a deployment. Remove the assignment from the workspace when you no longer need it.

docs/self-managed/concepts/multi-tenancy/physical-tenants.md: ## Physical Tenants in Camunda Hub
docs/self-managed/concepts/multi-tenancy/physical-tenants.md: In Camunda Hub, each Physical Tenant is an [environment](/components/concepts/environments.md), and teams deploy to it instead of to the cluster. The `default` Physical Tenant is named after the cluster, and every other Physical Tenant is named after its tenant ID.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: ### Environments
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: In 8.10, teams deploy to [environments](/components/concepts/environments.md) instead of the clusters connected to a project.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: What happens when you upgrade:
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: - Camunda Hub creates environments from the clusters in your `camunda.hub.clusters` configuration. On a cluster at version 8.10 or later, each cluster has an environment for the `default` Physical Tenant, plus one for each Physical Tenant you declare. See [physical tenants](/self-managed/components/hub/configuration/properties.md#physical-tenants).
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: - During the data migration, Camunda Hub assigns to each workspace an environment for every cluster that its projects used at any deployment stage, and for every cluster that its IDP projects used. If several projects share a workspace, the workspace gets all their clusters, and each one is assigned once. Existing projects can continue to deploy to the clusters they used before. For a cluster at version 8.10 or later, the environment is backed by the `default` Physical Tenant and keeps its workspace assignments.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: - Projects no longer have their own deployment stages or connected clusters. A project can deploy to every environment assigned to its workspace.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: - Workspaces you create after the upgrade start without environments. An organization admin must [assign environments to the workspace](/components/hub/organization/manage-environments/assign-environments.md).
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: - A cluster or Physical Tenant that is no longer in the configuration, for example because its ID changed, stays in Camunda Hub with the status **Not reported** while a workspace uses it. Reassign the workspace to another environment, or remove the assignment, when you no longer need it.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: Before you upgrade, review the following:
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: | Task                                    | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: | :-------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: | (Optional) Tag a cluster with `prod`    | Do this only if you want Camunda Hub to treat the environments of a cluster as production environments. Camunda Hub identifies them by the `prod` tag in `camunda.hub.clusters[].tags`, and the project deployment policy applies to them. Without the tag, no production restriction applies. |
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: | Add a `readiness` address to components | Camunda Hub determines the status of an environment from the `urls.readiness` address of its components. Without it, the status is **Unknown**.                                                                                                                                                |
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: Camunda Hub reads the cluster configuration at startup. After you change it, perform a rolling restart.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: #### Deployments to production environments
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: In earlier versions, only administrators could deploy to the production stage of a project by default. In 8.10, this default no longer applies to environments. Any workspace admin or editor with deployment privileges in the cluster can deploy to an environment tagged `prod`. Access to production is controlled by which environments are assigned to the workspace and by the deployment permissions in the cluster.
docs/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md: To require an approved project snapshot for production deployments, turn on **Require approval of project snapshots to deploy to production environments** in the [project deployment settings](/components/hub/workspace/modeler/modeler-settings.md#project-deployment).
docs/self-managed/upgrade/helm/890-to-8100.md: The migration also carries the clusters that your projects used into Camunda Hub as environments and assigns them to their workspaces. See [environments](/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md#environments).
docs/self-managed/upgrade/helm/890-to-8100.md: 1. In Camunda Hub, click **Environments** in the left navigation, and confirm that the expected environments appear with the correct status. Assign environments to any workspace that you create after the upgrade. See [environments](/self-managed/upgrade/components/890-to-8100.md#environments).
